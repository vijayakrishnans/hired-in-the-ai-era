There's nothing wrong with using AI to look for a job. Employers use it too. The problem is using it in a way that removes the one thing a reader was looking for, which is you.

Generated application text tends to sound the same: smooth, enthusiastic and specific about nothing. Hiring managers read a lot of it. You don't want to be the next one.

## Where it helps

**Understanding a job description.** Paste one in and ask what the role seems to actually need, what's probably a nice-to-have and what you should find out before applying.

**Researching a company.** Use it to get oriented and to generate questions, then check anything that matters against the company's own material. Models can be out of date, and they can be simply wrong.

**Finding gaps.** Give it your résumé and a job description, and ask what a sceptical reader would find missing. You don't have to agree. It's a cheap second opinion.

**Practising.** Have it play a tough interviewer, and ask it to push on your weak answers. More on this in [the article on interviewing](/posts/interviewing-in-the-ai-era).

**Editing.** Ask it to find what's unclear, repetitive or wordy in something you wrote. Keep your words, and take its notes.

## Where to be careful

**Never let it invent anything.** Tools will happily add a skill you don't have or round a result up. Read every line, and ask yourself if you could defend it in an interview. A false claim on an application can end the process, or a job later on.

**Don't let it write the part that's meant to be you.** Why this company, why you and what you've actually done should come from your own head, even if it comes out rough the first time.

**Don't mass-apply with automated tools.** They're tempting, and they usually produce a lot of poorly matched applications. Some can also get you flagged, and some apply in your name to jobs you'd never want.

**Watch what you paste in.** Check how a tool handles your data before giving it personal details, and don't paste in confidential material from a current or past employer.

**Check the employer's rules.** Some employers state what's allowed in applications and assessments. Where they do, follow it.

## A way of working

1. **Start with your own rough notes.** Write down, badly and quickly, the true things you want to say.
2. **Ask for structure and criticism,** rather than a finished draft.
3. **Rewrite it yourself,** reading it aloud. If you wouldn't say a sentence to another person, change it.
4. **Add one thing a tool couldn't know.** A conversation you had with someone there, a product detail you noticed as a real customer, or a specific problem you've solved that looks like theirs.
5. **Check every fact.** Dates, numbers, names, and the name of the company. That last one goes wrong more often than you'd think.

## How to tell if it worked

Cover the name at the top. Could this application have been sent by anyone else with a similar background? If so, it needs more of you in it.

Then spend the time you saved somewhere a tool can't go for you: talking to the people who do the job you want.
